# MLB DFS — Guide & Cheat Sheet

MLB is the highest-variance DFS sport. A great hitter fails 7 out of 10 at-bats. You are not picking players who *will* hit; you're picking spots where the odds are tilted in your favor and letting volume do the rest. Think in **environments** (park, weather, pitcher, lineup spot), not in names.

---

## 1. The Basics

### Formats
| Format | Goal | Build style |
|---|---|---|
| **Cash** (50/50s, double-ups, H2H) | Beat ~half the field | Safe floors, elite pitching, top-of-order bats, low variance |
| **GPP / Tournaments** | Beat almost everyone | Stacks, correlation, leverage vs. ownership, accept busts |
| **Showdown / Single game** | One game, captain 1.5x | Correlate heavily; captain is your biggest decision |

### Roster (DraftKings classic)
- 2 P, 1 C, 1 1B, 1 2B, 1 3B, 1 SS, 3 OF — $50,000 salary
- FanDuel: 1 P, 1 C/1B, 1 2B, 1 3B, 1 SS, 3 OF, 1 UTIL — $35,000

### Scoring you actually need to know (DK)
**Hitters:** 1B = 3, 2B = 5, 3B = 8, HR = 10, RBI = 2, R = 2, BB/HBP = 2, SB = 5
**Pitchers:** IP = 2.25, K = 2, W = 4, ER = −2, H/BB against = −0.6, CG = 2.5, shutout/no-hitter bonuses

**What this means:**
- A home run with a runner on is ~16+ points. Power is king.
- Walks (2) are worth two-thirds of a single (3). High-OBP hitters have a floor.
- Stolen bases (5) are quietly huge — a single + SB = a double.
- For pitchers, **strikeouts and innings** drive the score. Wins are a bonus, not a plan.

## 2. Pitching — Your Foundation

Pitching is ~40% of your salary and the most *predictable* part of the slate. Nail it first.

### The stats that matter (in order)
1. **K% (strikeout rate)** — the #1 predictor of DFS points. 25%+ is good, 30%+ is elite.
2. **Opponent K%** — pitch against teams that strike out a lot. Check the *projected lineup's* K% vs. that pitcher's handedness.
3. **xFIP / SIERA** — better than ERA at predicting the future. ERA tells you what happened; SIERA tells you what should have.
4. **Vegas** — implied run total against him (under 3.5 is great) and moneyline (−150 or better for win equity).
5. **Pitch count / innings** — is he going 6+? Check recent pitch counts. Guys on a limit cap your ceiling.
6. **Park** — pitcher-friendly parks (Oracle, Petco, T-Mobile, Citi) boost floor.
7. **Umpire** — some umps have noticeably bigger zones (more Ks, fewer walks). Minor edge, but free.
8. **Walk rate (BB%)** — under 7% is clean. Walks = baserunners = runs and short outings.

### Rules of thumb
- **Cash:** pay for the safest high-K arm on the slate. Don't get cute.
- **GPP:** one stud + one cheaper arm with K upside in a good spot, OR two mid-tier arms to free up money for a big stack.
- Fade good pitchers vs. patient, low-K lineups (Astros, Guardians types). Target bad pitchers vs. anyone.
- A pitcher's **season K%** matters more than his last 3 starts.

## 3. Hitting — Where the Ceiling Lives

### The Big Five (check every hitter against these)
1. **Lineup spot** — Batting 1–5 gets ~0.6 more plate appearances/game than 7–9. Over a season that's an extra 100 PAs. Always confirm lineups before lock. Avoid 7–9 hitters in cash.
2. **Opposing pitcher** — Target high xFIP, low K%, high HR/FB, high hard-hit% allowed. Bad pitchers vs. good hitters is the whole game.
3. **Platoon splits** — Most hitters are much better vs. the opposite hand (LHB vs. RHP, RHB vs. LHP). Look up **wOBA vs. L/R** — don't assume.
4. **Park factor** — Coors is a different sport. Great Rivers, Yankee Stadium (LHB), Citizens Bank, Globe Life (roof closed), Cincinnati also play up. Oracle, Petco, Kauffman play down.
5. **Vegas implied team total** — 5+ runs = target. 3.5 or under = fade (unless it's a leverage play in GPP).

### Stats that predict power (what you want)
| Stat | What it tells you | Good |
|---|---|---|
| **ISO** (SLG − AVG) | Raw power | .200+ |
| **wOBA / wRC+** | Overall offensive value | .350+ / 120+ |
| **Barrel %** | Hard, well-angled contact | 10%+ |
| **Hard-hit %** | Exit velo 95+ | 45%+ |
| **HR/FB** | Fly balls leaving the yard | 18%+ |
| **K%** | Contact ability (floor) | under 22% |
| **BB%** | On-base floor | 10%+ |

### Weather — the free edge most people skip
- **Wind blowing out** at 10+ mph = HR bump. **Wind blowing in** = fade the HR upside.
- **Temperature:** Hot and humid = ball carries. Cold (under 55°F) = suppressed offense.
- **Rain / postponement risk:** A PPD after lock = zero points. Check the radar and be willing to pivot late.
- **Dome/roof:** Neutral weather, but note roof-open vs. closed in Texas, Arizona, Houston, Milwaukee.

## 4. Stacking — The GPP Engine

Runs are correlated. When the #2 hitter homers with two on, the #1 and #9 hitters score too. A **stack** is 3–5 hitters from the same lineup, ideally consecutive (2-3-4-5 or 1-2-3-4).

### Why it wins
A single big inning produces 30–50 points across a stack. You can't win a large GPP with 9 uncorrelated 10-point hitters.

### How to build
- **Primary stack:** 4–5 bats from your best offensive environment (high implied total, bad pitcher, good park, weather).
- **Secondary stack:** 2–3 bats from a second good spot.
- **One-offs:** fill with cheap, top-of-order, high-OBP bats — or a leverage play (see below).
- Stack **consecutive** hitters. 1-2-3-4 or 2-3-4-5 beats 1-4-7-9.
- **Stack against the pitcher you're fading**, not just "the Rockies at home."

### Common stack targets
- Team implied 5+ runs
- Facing a pitcher with xFIP 4.75+ or K% under 20%
- Bullpen behind him is bad (late-inning runs count too)
- Lefty-heavy lineup vs. RHP with big reverse splits, or vice versa

## 5. Ownership & Leverage (GPP only)

Projected ownership matters as much as projected points in tournaments.

- **Chalk** (25%+ owned) is usually chalk for a reason. Playing it is fine — but stacking *only* chalk means you need a miracle to beat thousands of identical lineups.
- **Leverage** = a play whose ceiling is similar to the chalk but at a fraction of the ownership. Second-best stack on the slate at 4% owned can be a better bet than the best stack at 35%.
- Ask: *"If this stack hits, how many people do I pass?"*
- Coors is always the highest-owned stack. Sometimes pay up for it, sometimes fade for leverage — but never fade it *just because* it's popular.
- In multi-entry, spread your primary stacks across 3–4 different teams rather than 20 versions of one.

---

## 6. Bankroll & Process

- **Never** put more than 5–10% of your bankroll on a single slate. MLB variance will wipe out a hot hand.
- Cash vs. GPP split: beginners 70/30, experienced 50/50 or lower.
- Track every entry: date, contest, ROI, what you did, what you'd change. Patterns show up after 100+ slates, not 10.
- Judge decisions, not results. A 4-run Coors stack that gets shut out was still the right play if the process was right.
- Start with **smaller slates** (afternoon, 4–6 games) — fewer variables, easier to learn.

## 7. Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts |
|---|---|
| Chasing last night's box score | Yesterday's 3-HR game has near-zero predictive value |
| Ignoring lineup confirmation | Your $5,200 hitter isn't playing = 0 points |
| Playing 7–9 hitters in cash | Fewest plate appearances on the field |
| Using ERA to evaluate pitchers | ERA lags; use K%, SIERA, xFIP |
| Fading Coors out of principle | It's the best environment in the sport; fade only for leverage, with intent |
| Roster-spot-hunting weak positions | Catcher is bad for everyone; punt it and spend elsewhere |
| Not checking the bullpen | Late innings score too; a good SP with a terrible bullpen is a lower-ceiling stack target |

---

## 8. Pre-Lock Checklist

- [ ] Lineups confirmed for every hitter (and batting order)
- [ ] Both pitchers confirmed; no late scratches
- [ ] Weather checked: wind direction, temp, rain risk, roof status
- [ ] Vegas totals & lines re-checked (they move)
- [ ] Every hitter is 1–6 in the order (cash) or part of a stack logic (GPP)
- [ ] Stacks are 3–5 consecutive hitters
- [ ] Ownership reviewed — do I have a leverage angle?
- [ ] Salary within $300 of the cap (unused money = wasted)
- [ ] Late swap plan for any game after the first lock

## ONE-PAGE CHEAT SHEET

**Slate order of operations:**
1. Vegas — sort teams by implied total, pitchers by opponent K% and ML.
2. Lock pitching — 1 safe stud (cash) or stud + upside arm (GPP).
3. Find 2–3 offensive environments: implied 5+, bad opposing SP, park, weather.
4. Stack consecutive hitters (1–5) from those spots.
5. Fill with cheap top-of-order bats; punt catcher.
6. Check ownership, adjust for leverage.
7. Confirm lineups & weather at lock.

**Pitcher targets:** K% 25%+ · opp K% 24%+ · SIERA under 3.75 · implied runs against under 3.75 · 90+ pitches recently · BB% under 8%

**Hitter targets:** batting 1–5 · ISO .200+ · wOBA vs. hand .340+ · Barrel% 10%+ · team implied 5+ · opp SP xFIP 4.5+

**Park tiers:**
- Elite hitting: Coors · Great American · Citizens Bank · Globe Life · Yankee Stadium (LHB) · Fenway
- Pitcher-friendly: Oracle · Petco · T-Mobile · Kauffman · Citi Field · Oakland/Sac

**Weather:** wind out 10+ = HR bump · temp 85+ = carries · under 55 = fade · always check rain

**Cash:** 2 safe arms, all hitters 1–5, high OBP, avoid stacks over 3, avoid rain games.
**GPP:** 4–5 man stack + 2–3 man stack, leverage on at least one, accept 1–2 duds.

**DK point math:** HR ≈ 14+ (with R + RBI) · 1B + SB = 8 · BB = 2 (floor) · Pitcher: 7 IP / 8 K / W ≈ 36
